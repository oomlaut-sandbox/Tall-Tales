# oomlaut Reader: making it irresistible to young readers

Brainstorm and strategy notes, 14 October 2026. No app code changes; ideas only.
Audience: children about 3 to 7, and the grown-ups who choose apps for them.

---

## 1. Where oomlaut sits

What the well-known kids' reading apps do (from general knowledge as of mid-2026; details may have
changed, worth a quick look before acting on any comparison):

| App | What it is | What it does well | Where it falls short for us |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Epic** | Huge subscription library (thousands of books, audiobooks, videos) for ages ~2–12 | Breadth; "Read-to-me" books; reading logs, badges, levels; teachers use it | Volume over curation; busy, game-like interface; badges and streaks can feel like a points machine |
| **Vooks** | Animated storybooks, closer to gentle cartoons | Calm motion, narration, read-along text | Watching more than reading; little for the child to do |
| **Readmio** | Parents read real books aloud; the app adds matching sound effects and music | Turns the parent into the narrator; very warm idea | Built around physical books; depends on the parent reading |
| **Homer** | Structured learn-to-read path (phonics, sight words) | Clear progression, personalised path | Lessons and levels; feels like school; less about loving a book |
| **Duolingo ABC** | Free bite-sized learn-to-read lessons | Short sessions, strong phonics, friendly characters | A lesson app, not a library; gamified at heart |

**oomlaut's distinct strengths**
- **Real art, made by an illustrator.** Every page looks like a book someone made by hand: felt, paper,
  pencil, watercolour. Most competitors look like apps; oomlaut looks like a book.
- **Your own voice.** Parents record each page and the child hears them every time. Readmio comes
  closest, but oomlaut keeps the recording *inside the book*, for grandparents far away, for bedtimes
  when a parent is travelling.
- **Make your own books.** Photos plus words become a real book with the same page turns, then print
  it as a PDF. That's a creative act few reading apps offer.
- **Calm by design.** One book floating on a quiet desk, page-by-page narration, no ads, no feeds,
  everything parental behind a gate. That calm is a selling point to parents. Protect it.
- **Learn to Read built into the stories**, not a separate lesson track.

**Positioning in one line:** *the beautiful, handmade storybook app where your family's voice lives
inside the books.* Compete on warmth and craft, not on how many books there are.

## 2. Ideas to add delight and engagement

Guiding rule: **reward the reading, not the screen time.** Every idea below should make a child want to
come back to a *book*, finish it, or read it with someone, not want to keep tapping.

### A. Things to discover inside the pictures
- **Hidden hotspots.** A few tappable things per page: the unicorn's tail wiggles, a raindrop giggles,
  Pip splashes. The art and the hotspot data already exist in the reader, so this mostly needs
  Jörg's ideas and a few sounds.
- **"Find it" moments** on a re-read: "Can you find the little snail?" A gentle second-read activity
  that rewards looking closely at the art.
- **Hidden pebble.** The felt pebble mascot hides somewhere in each book; finding it is the reward.
  It brings children back to old books, and it's charming rather than compulsive.

### B. Progress and rewards that stay kind
- **A sticker book.** Finishing a book earns a felt sticker of one of its characters, kept in a
  sticker album the child can open. One sticker per book (maybe one more for finding the pebble),
  so there's nothing to grind.
- **A bookshelf that fills up.** Finished books get a little ribbon on My Library. Seeing "my books"
  grow is a quiet, real sense of progress.
- **Streaks, kindly.** If there are streaks at all: count *days we read together this week*, never
  a streak that breaks and scolds. No "you lost your streak!" messages. Better still, a weekly
  "reading garden" that grows a flower for each day read and never wilts.
- **Celebrate the end of a book**, not the time spent: the pebble cheers on The End page, maybe a
  short music sting (from the sound-effects plan).

### C. Personalisation
- **The child's name in the app** (set by the parent): "Welcome back, Mia!" said by the mascot or
  narrator.
- **"Starring you" books** in Create a Story: templates where a parent drops in the child's photo
  and name ("Mia and the Pond of Stars").
- **A favourite voice.** Let the child pick Hannah, Frank or Mum/Dad (recordings) with big face
  icons, not words.
- **Background skins** (already planned) as a small reward or choice: felt desk, night sky, garden.

### D. Replay value
- **Different ways to read the same book:** listen; read with me (Page by Page); "you read, I'll
  help" (Learn to Read); a sound-effects version (from the store pipeline). Each is a reason to
  return.
- **Bedtime mode:** a dimmer, warmer desk, slower narration, no sound effects, and the book closes
  itself gently at The End with a "good night" from the pebble.
- **Read it again, the parent's version.** After the narrator version, offer the recorded parent
  version when there is one ("Hear Dad read it?").

### E. Discovery (for children who can't read menus yet)
- **Picture-first choosing.** Children choose by cover. Make covers bigger, and a tap on a cover in
  the Store should play a short spoken title ("Falling, a story about a raindrop") before the
  details box.
- **"Read me something!"** One big button (or the mascot) that opens a surprise book the child
  hasn't finished, for the youngest users who can't navigate.
- **Shelves by feeling**, chosen by parents in the Store: bedtime, funny, feelings, animals. Shelves
  already exist; the names do the work.

### F. The mascot's job (when it arrives)
- Welcome at launch (a few seconds of something funny, then out of the way).
- Point at things a pre-reader can't read: "Tap the corner to turn the page!" with a wiggle toward
  the dog-ear, the first few times only.
- Cheer at The End; hand out the sticker.
- Hide in each book (see A).
- **Never** nag, beg the child to come back, or appear during the story itself.

### G. Reading with someone
- **Record together.** A child records a page too (copying the parent), so it can be played back
  in their own voice: lovely for early readers.
- **Send a voice to Grandma.** The share invite could carry the family's recorded version so a
  grandparent hears the child read (needs accounts and storage; a bigger bet).
- **Grown-up corner** (behind the parent gate): what was read this week, which words were tapped
  in Learn to Read, and suggested next books. Gentle insight for parents, nothing shown to the child.

### H. Accessibility
- Text size already exists; add a **dyslexia-friendly book font option** (e.g. a rounded, evenly
  spaced sans serif) alongside Serif and Helvetica.
- **Higher-contrast text page** option (white page, darker ink).
- **Captions for all spoken prompts**, and VoiceOver labels on every button (mostly present).
- **Left-handed page turning**: let the dog-ear sit bottom-left as an option.
- **Reduce motion** setting: replace the peel and slides with simple fades.

## 3. Barriers for very young and pre-readers (and fixes)

| Barrier today | Why it matters | How to lower it |
|---|---|---|
| **Text-based menus** (Settings, Playback Options, Book Store details) | A 3-year-old can't read "Playback Settings" | Picture icons for child-facing choices; speak the item when tapped; keep text menus for grown-ups behind the gate |
| **The start page asks for a choice** (My Library / Book Store) | Pre-readers can't read either label | Spoken labels on tap, or let the coloured squares carry pictures (bookshelf, shop); the mascot can point |
| **Page-turn needs the small dog-ear or a precise swipe** | Small fingers miss small targets | A bigger invisible tap zone on the right edge; the first-time hint from the mascot; a forgiving swipe threshold |
| **"Hold to edit," select modes, parent gates** | Confusing or accidentally triggered | Already gated; make sure the child-facing screens have no long-press surprises |
| **Book Store purchases and memberships** | Must never be reachable by a child | Keep all buying behind the parent gate (already the plan); no prices on the child's side; nothing that pushes the child to ask for purchases |
| **Learn to Read only speaks single words** | Pre-readers need the sounds, not just the word | Already partly there (word-part clips); expand with letter sounds and "say it slowly" |
| **Small text in captions and tags** (Free, 2/16) | Not for children anyway | Fine; keep child-facing text big and few |
| **Quiet when sound effects are off by default** | Some children need the audio cue that something happened | Keep effects off by default (good for calm), but make the very first book open with a gentle sound or narration so the child knows it's working |

## 4. What to avoid (dark patterns)

- No breaking streaks, guilt messages, or "your pet is sad" mechanics.
- No loot boxes, random rewards, or currencies to collect and spend.
- No timers, countdowns, or "only today" offers, ever.
- No push notifications aimed at the child; reminders only if the parent asks for them.
- No in-app purchases or ads visible to the child; purchases only behind the parent gate.
- No endless autoplay into the next book; finishing a book should feel finished, with a calm
  "read again or choose another" (as The End page already does).
- No collecting data on children beyond what the family needs; be clear about this in the
  App Store listing (it also matters for the Kids Category rules).

## 5. Priorities

### Quick wins (days, mostly design and content)
1. **Spoken labels** for the start page squares and Store covers (tap = hear it).
2. **Bigger page-turn tap zone** plus a one-time "tap the corner" hint.
3. **Hotspots in the three free books** (a few per book, using the existing hotspot support).
4. **Celebration on The End page**: a short sound and a little animation.
5. **Bedtime mode** (dim, warm, slower, no effects).
6. **Ribbons on finished books** in My Library.

### Medium (a week or two each)
7. **Sticker album** (one felt sticker per finished book).
8. **The felt pebble mascot**, starting with the welcome, the hint and the cheer (needs Jörg's poses).
9. **Child's name and favourite-voice picker**, set up by the parent.
10. **Reduce motion, high-contrast and dyslexia-friendly options.**
11. **Grown-up corner** behind the gate: what was read, words tapped.

### Bigger bets (weeks, need accounts, storage or new content)
12. **Hide-the-pebble in every book**, plus "find it" second reads (needs art per book).
13. **"Starring you" templates** in Create a Story.
14. **Sound-effects and music editions** of store books (already in the store plan).
15. **Send a family voice to a grandparent** (recordings travel with the share invite).
16. **Multi-language editions** (parked for later; see earlier notes).

### Suggested order
Do quick wins 1–4 first: they directly help the youngest readers and need no new art. Then the
sticker album and the mascot together, since the mascot can hand out the stickers. Leave the bigger
bets for when the Store pipeline and accounts are in place.
